# Future Improvements (Parked)

1. **AI Summary improvements** — Better prompting, structured output, improved formatting in the PDF (page 2)
2. **Client file upload website** — Web portal for attorneys to upload WAV files + ICM XML directly, rather than sharing via Dropbox/drive
3. **"How to use" PDF** — Sleek, branded PDF guide replacing README.txt that explains the deliverable package to non-technical users
4. **Offline preamble stripping (rebuild from scratch)** — Every recording opens with the jail phone system's preamble (facility announcement, "this call is being recorded", accept-call prompts), mirrored on both channels. Today it is only removed when a summarization model flags it (`backend/system_audio.py`, Gemini structured pass or the Gemma `SYSTEM_AUDIO:` line). Want a deterministic, engine-agnostic pass that runs before summarization and works with no model call: detect the preamble boundary from the transcript (and ideally the audio) and drop or label those turns. An earlier heuristic attempt (cross-channel text matching in `transcription/base.py`) was abandoned; do not resurrect it, design fresh.
5. **Parakeet turn merging / word interleaving (rebuild from scratch)** — The local engine transcribes each channel separately and `parakeet_engine._merge_segments` stitches the two word streams into speaker turns with a simple gap heuristic. Interruptions, overlapping speech, and back-channel words ("yeah", "uh-huh") produce wrong turn boundaries and mis-ordered turns. Needs a proper word-level interleave that splits segments on interruptions and keeps both channels time-consistent. An earlier WIP existed but was never finished; start clean, and note the local engine itself may change (see item 6).
6. **Revisit the local-only mode** — Parakeet (FluidAudio CoreML) and Gemma 4 E2B (mlx-lm) were the first local engines; a vllm-mlx / OpenAI-compatible-server approach was also tried and dropped. Parked until the codebase is modular enough that swapping engines is cheap. Likely different models next time. Anything done for items 4 and 5 should stay engine-agnostic so it survives that swap.
7. **Note caps, decided once** — Four unrelated limits currently decide how many notes a reader sees for a call: the prompt's per-tier guidance (`prompts.SUMMARY_NOTE_GUIDANCE`: LOW about 3, MEDIUM at most 6, HIGH about 12; advisory only, `summaries.py` imports it but never enforces it), the hard max of 21 (`SUMMARY_NOTE_HARD_MAX`, enforced in `_curate_note_items`), the summary-sheet page budget (`summaries.SUMMARY_PAGE_LIMITS`: LOW and MEDIUM one page, HIGH three; `_fit_note_items_to_page_budget` drops the weakest notes until the sheet fits, so in practice a MEDIUM or LOW call keeps 5 or 6 notes depending on quote length and a HIGH call can keep all 21), and the case report's silent `view.cues[:8]` slice in `delivery/case_report._build_call_card` (there since the first case-report commit, no recorded rationale; on the demo batch five of six HIGH cards are cut with nothing saying so). Decide one policy: which limit is the real per-tier cap, whether the page budget should shape the content or only the pagination, and whether every surface (summary sheet, index row, viewer rail, report card) shows the same set. Then enforce it in one place, and either show every kept note on the card or say how many are missing.
